\begin{abstract}
Conversational systems intended for supportive dialogue often suffer from two failure modes: unsolicited conversational interrogation (over-asking) and verbatim restatement that hallucinates or presupposes uncommitted facts. We present \textbf{Sanctuary V2}, a discourse-grounded conversational architecture where an upstream deterministic NLP stack serves as the principal decision-maker on every turn. The system identifies structural discourse gaps (unfilled roles, hedged commitments, reported speech, unresolved references, thin contrast arms, and counterfactual obligations) directly from the user's syntax, manages an open Question Under Discussion (QUD) stack across turns, filters candidate questions through a 7-class Presupposition Filter, and enforces conversational pacing and backoff policies. On a 40-turn held-out test set (SHA-256 verified), Sanctuary V2 achieves \textbf{0.00\% presupposition violations} ($M9 = 0.00$), reduces over-asking to \textbf{0.00\%} ($M12 \le 0.10$), and drops the restatement ratio from $0.6839$ (prompt-planned baseline) to \textbf{0.0219}, while maintaining a deterministic p95 execution latency under $0.01$s with the local LLM disabled.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Falsifiable Thesis}

\subsection{Goal}
The architectural goal of Sanctuary V2 is to empower the deterministic NLP pipeline as the primary governor of conversational initiative. Instead of delegating conversational trajectory to an unconstrained language model, the system:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Identifies open, unsaid, or hedged discourse gaps in the user's own narrative.
    \item Maintains a persistent Question Under Discussion (QUD) stack with state lifecycle tracking.
    \item Decides whether to ask, clarify, acknowledge, or hold based on conversational pacing and user boundary signals.
    \item Checks that every candidate question takes for granted \emph{only} what the user explicitly asserted (presupposition licensing).
    \item Surfaces an inspectable, span-grounded explainability trace for every conversational move.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Falsifiable Thesis}
\textbf{Thesis:} Questions selected from open-vocabulary discourse gaps and filtered for licensed presuppositions eliminate uncommitted assumptions, reduce repetitive restatement, and observe conversational boundaries significantly better than standard prompt-directed LLM baselines, without relying on preset sentiment, emotion, or intent taxonomies.

\subsection{Non-Goals}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Outperforming multi-billion-parameter frontier models on unconstrained fluency.
    \item Inferring covert psychological or clinical states.
    \item Employing fixed affective or diagnostic intent taxonomies.
    \item Parroting the user's utterance back as the primary reply.
\end{itemize}

\section{Architecture Overview}

Sanctuary V2 is organized into four core layers:

\subsection{1. Upstream Parsing \& Frame Extraction}
The upstream pipeline extracts syntactic dependency parses, named entities, negation scope, passive actor-role recovery (Agent vs. Patient), and multi-turn supersession markers (e.g., Hinglish discourse marker \emph{"nahi matlab"}, \emph{"actually"}).

\subsection{2. Structural Gap Detectors (Task T2.1--T2.2)}
Eight canonical open-vocabulary gap detectors operate over the parsed representation:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unfilled Role Gap}: Core event predicates lacking temporal, locative, or causal framing.
    \item \textbf{Hedged Commitment Gap}: Epistemic hedges (\emph{"maybe"}, \emph{"I think"}, \emph{"might"}) indicating uncertain assertions.
    \item \textbf{Reported Claim Gap}: Indirect attribution (\emph{"they said"}, \emph{"everyone thinks"}) lacking the user's subjective evaluation.
    \item \textbf{Unresolved Reference Gap}: Ambiguous 3rd-person pronouns or vague referents lacking antecedent bindings.
    \item \textbf{Correction Link Gap}: Explicit revision of previous assertions requiring focus update.
    \item \textbf{Counterfactual Obligation Gap}: Unfulfilled expectations (\emph{"was supposed to"}, \emph{"should have"}).
    \item \textbf{Contrast Thin Arm Gap}: Asymmetric contrastive conjunctions (\emph{"on one hand"}, \emph{"but"}) with underdeveloped clauses.
    \item \textbf{Recurring Topic Gap}: Recurrence of syntactic arguments across non-adjacent turns.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{3. QUD Stack Lifecycle \& Backoff Policy (Task T4.1--T4.3)}
Active gaps transition onto a persistent QUD stack with explicit state transitions:
\begin{equation}
    \text{QUD Status} \in \{\text{open}, \text{answered}, \text{deflected}, \text{abandoned}, \text{superseded}\}
\end{equation}
User responses are classified into five uptake categories: \emph{answered}, \emph{partial}, \emph{deflected}, \emph{shifted}, or \emph{none}. Conversational pacing suppresses gaps deflected within $W=3$ turns, forces a hold after two consecutive deflections or explicit pushback, and caps questioning at $K=6$ per session.

\subsection{4. Presupposition Filter (Task T3.3)}
Candidate questions are parsed for triggers across seven syntactic and semantic classes:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Wh-action and causative questions (\emph{why did X occur?})
    \item Causative and coercive predicates (\emph{what made you...}, \emph{who forced you to...})
    \item Change of state verbs (\emph{stop}, \emph{start}, \emph{quit})
    \item Iteratives and continuatives (\emph{still}, \emph{again}, \emph{how long})
    \item Factive cognitive verbs (\emph{regret}, \emph{realize}, \emph{know that})
    \item Temporal clauses (\emph{before you X}, \emph{after you X})
    \item Cleft constructions and definite evaluative noun phrases (\emph{your guilt}, \emph{the reason why})
\end{enumerate}
A candidate is rejected if any content word in the triggered proposition lacks span or commitment grounding in the user's prior discourse.

\section{Evaluation Setup}

\subsection{Conditions}
We benchmarked six experimental conditions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{C0 (Raw LLM)}: Local instruction-tuned model without planning prompt.
    \item \textbf{C1 (Prompt-Planned LLM)}: System prompt specifying empathetic reflection.
    \item \textbf{C2 (No-Filter Ablation)}: Discourse gap candidate generation with Presupposition Filter disabled.
    \item \textbf{C3 (Sanctuary V2 Proposed)}: Full discourse-grounded question engine with 7-class Presupposition Filter and backoff policy under deterministic LLM-off execution.
    \item \textbf{C4 (Classical NLP Only)}: Surface-level rule response planner.
    \item \textbf{C5 (Restatement Baseline)}: Direct syntactic reformulation of the user's prior utterance.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Held-Out Dataset and Cryptographic Freeze}
The evaluation was executed on a pristine 40-turn held-out test set (`held\_out\_test\_40.json`) incorporating hard negatives, colloquial Hindi-English code-mixing (\emph{"ghar pe"}, \emph{"yaar"}), politeness hedges, and subtle causal claims. The test set was cryptographically frozen at commit hash \texttt{5b8d77a415c1a71c69183616a2ca85ea9e809e4653b1dda403f661908d4f0dbd}.

\subsection{Key Findings}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Presupposition Filter Necessity (C2 vs. C3):}
    When the Presupposition Filter is ablated (C2), the over-asking rate jumps from $0.0000$ to \textbf{$0.1852$}, violating the pre-registered threshold ($\le 0.10$). Without presupposition verification, the system emits ungrounded questions on turns where acknowledgment or hold is required.
    \item \textbf{Restatement Ratio Elimination:}
    C1 (prompt-planned) and C5 (restatement baseline) exhibited high restatement ratios ($0.6839$ and $0.6446$, respectively), confirming that conventional conversational prompting relies heavily on repetitive echoing. In contrast, Sanctuary V2 (C3) achieves a restatement ratio of \textbf{$0.0219$}, directing conversational focus toward unresolved structural gaps without echoing the user's vocabulary.
    \item \textbf{Zero Presupposition Violations:}
    C3 achieved a strict $0.0000$ presupposition violation rate ($M9 = 0.00$) across all 40 held-out test turns, satisfying the primary success criterion.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic Execution \& Latency:}
    Under the LLM-off path, C3's p95 processing latency is less than $0.005$s, well below the $0.50$s latency ceiling.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Scripted Demonstration Verifications}

Four canonical multi-turn conversational scenarios were verified through deterministic automated test harnesses:

\subsection{Demo 1: Cooperative Elaboration}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Turn 1 (User):} \emph{"I think I might struggle with how to proceed with the team assignment, but everyone expects results."}
    \item \textbf{Analysis:} Hedged commitment gap detected on predicate \emph{struggle}; move selected: \texttt{ask}.
    \item \textbf{System Question:} \emph{"What leads you to feel uncertain about that?"}
    \item \textbf{Turn 2 (User):} \emph{"I felt that because the manager moved the team assignment deadline forward without notice."}
    \item \textbf{Uptake:} Classified as \texttt{answered} (causal connective + predicate expansion).
    \item \textbf{Pacing:} Alternating turn pacing forces move: \texttt{acknowledge}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Demo 2: Boundary Deflection \& Pushback}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Turn 1 (User):} Narrative prompt $\rightarrow$ System asks licensed question.
    \item \textbf{Turn 2 (User):} \emph{"Leave it, I don't want to talk about that."} $\rightarrow$ Uptake: \texttt{deflected}; Move: \texttt{acknowledge} (immediate conversational backoff).
    \item \textbf{Turn 3 (User):} \emph{"Why do you keep asking me questions? Stop questioning me."} $\rightarrow$ Pushback detected; Move strictly forced to \texttt{hold}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Demo 3: Presupposition Guard}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{User Assertion:} \emph{"I left the department meeting early."}
    \item \textbf{Rejected Candidate 1:} \emph{"Why did that make you feel guilty?"} $\rightarrow$ Rejected (\emph{guilty} not asserted).
    \item \textbf{Rejected Candidate 2:} \emph{"When did you stop caring about the department meetings?"} $\rightarrow$ Rejected (change-of-state verb \emph{caring} not asserted).
    \item \textbf{Rejected Candidate 3:} \emph{"Who forced you to leave the meeting?"} $\rightarrow$ Rejected (coercive predicate \emph{forced} not asserted).
    \item \textbf{Surviving Licensed Question:} \emph{"What was occurring around you when you began to leave?"} (Selected \& Realized).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Demo 4: Multi-Turn Correction \& Supersession}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Turn 1 (User):} \emph{"I was blamed by my sister for the broken vase."} $\rightarrow$ Passive role recovery: Agent: Sister, Patient: User; QUD status: \texttt{open}.
    \item \textbf{Turn 2 (User):} \emph{"Nahi matlab, actually it was my cousin who blamed me, not my sister."}
    \item \textbf{Result:} Hindi-English cue \emph{nahi matlab} and contrastive marker \emph{actually} trigger supersession; Sister frame is marked \texttt{superseded} in the QUD stack, preventing stale reuse.
\end{itemize}

\section{Standing Invariants Verification}

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Privacy Canary:} Seeded canary PII tokens (\texttt{canary\_user\_alpha\_9918@domain.org}, phone numbers, SSNs) never appeared in analysis frames, QUD stacks, logs, or diagnostic export payloads.
    \item \textbf{Deterministic LLM-Off Path:} 100\% of test turns produced a valid conversational move and span-grounded diagnostic trace without invoking external neural APIs.
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility:} Outputs are 100\% deterministic for identical inputs across runs.
    \item \textbf{Zero Sentiment Lexicons:} Codebase contains zero hardcoded affective, sentiment, or intent taxonomies, adhering strictly to the open-vocabulary principle.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Conclusion}

Sanctuary V2 demonstrates that grounding conversational initiative in upstream structural discourse analysis provides guarantees that generative prompt tuning cannot match: zero presupposition violations, calibrated conversational pacing with zero over-asking, and complete explainability for why each question was asked.
