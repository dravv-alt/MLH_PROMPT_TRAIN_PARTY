## HOW TO WRITE THE COLD EMAIL

The email should be 5 sentences. Not more. This is the structure:

Sentence 1: One sentence on who you are and what you built. Name the hackathon. Be specific.

Sentence 2: One sentence on the specific technical contribution that aligns with their work. Use their vocabulary. For Marieke Wichers: "We apply critical slowing down signatures to individual narrative state trajectories." For Dirk Hermans: "We are trying to operationalize narrative coherence as a measurable outcome in AI-mediated conversation." For Tanmoy Chakraborty: "We built a privacy-by-distillation architecture for an emotional AI companion."

Sentence 3: One sentence naming the exact problem you are stuck on. Be honest. "The gap is that we have no validated coherence measurement instrument for this context." Researchers like honest problem statements more than polished pitches.

Sentence 4: One sentence asking a specific question. Not "can we collaborate." A specific intellectual question. "Would the NaCCS instrument be applicable to multi-turn conversational data?" "Is critical slowing down detection from 10-turn windows statistically reliable?"

Sentence 5: Offer something. "I can share the full technical architecture document if useful." Researchers want to see the work, not just hear about it.

Do not attach anything to the first email. Do not mention collaboration, funding, or co-authorship. Just ask a genuine question about their specific work as it relates to a specific problem you have. That is what gets a response.
